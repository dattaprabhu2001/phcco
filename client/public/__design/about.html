About us

Who we are

India's first dedicated centre for computational oncology, established at IISc Bengaluru in 2023.

Indian Institute
of Science

PHCCO — the Param Hansa Centre for Computational Oncology — was established through a partnership between the Indian Institute of Science and Param Hansa Philanthropies.

We bring together mathematics, biology, medicine, engineering, and computation — applying systems biology, machine learning, multi-omics, mathematical modelling, and AI to understand how cancers develop, evolve, and respond to treatment.

In three years, PHCCO has built a community spanning researchers, clinicians, students, and partners. It has produced research across phenotypic plasticity, tumour evolution, cancer microenvironment, epigenomics, and digital pathology, and trained a first cohort of scientists who are beginning to define their own research directions.

Our vision

Our vision

To make computational oncology a genuine force for better cancer outcomes in India — and to establish India as a scientific leader in this field globally.

01

A distinct burden needs distinct science

India's cancer burden has unique tumour biology, late-stage presentation and limited access to precision treatment — at a scale that demands solutions which are affordable and clinically usable, not only scientifically elegant.

02

The window is open now

The convergence of AI, multi-omics, digital pathology and clinical data is creating a genuine opportunity to move computational oncology from scientific insight to clinical impact.

03

India can lead, not follow

The data opportunity, the talent base, and the research ecosystem are all in place. PHCCO is a bet on that potential — and on the scientists, students and collaborators making it real.

Strategic domains

Six domains we are building in

Where the centre concentrates its effort — from defining a scientific identity to closing the gap between computation and the clinic.

01

Scientific Leadership

Establish a globally recognised scientific identity in 2–3 computational oncology focus areas — not merely contributing to the field, but helping to define it.

02

Research Infrastructure & Data Ecosystem

Build the data and infrastructure foundations that make PHCCO's science possible at scale — and shareable across institutions.

03

Collaborations

Deepen and formalise partnerships — with clinical institutions, industry, and international collaborators — that translate science into real-world relevance and amplify reach.

04

Talent Pipeline

Build a training ecosystem that produces computational oncologists who are scientifically rigorous, interdisciplinary by instinct, and ready to lead.

05

Translational Momentum

Close the gap between computational insight and clinical application — demonstrating that PHCCO's science can change what happens for Indian cancer patients.

06

Publications & Visibility

Build the scientific record — a body of peer-reviewed work that establishes the centre’s contribution and makes it citable, visible and useful to the field.

Our work

What we do

Four activities, one purpose: turning quantitative insight into better cancer outcomes.

Research

Three interlocking themes — phenotypic plasticity and cellular heterogeneity; gene regulatory networks and cell-fate decision making; and tumour ecosystems, evolution and therapeutic strategies.

Train

A six-week immersive summer internship at IISc for students from engineering, biology, physics, computer science and medicine — three weeks of foundations, three weeks of mentored research.

Convene

The Workshop on Computational Oncology (WoCON) series, run with partner institutions in eleven cities, plus symposia and conferences that bring the field together.

Publish & share

Peer-reviewed research, visitor blogs from leading researchers worldwide, and podcast conversations that make the field legible beyond its specialists.
Impact

The impact we can bring

Computational oncology accelerates cancer research, supports precision medicine, reduces experimental costs, and facilitates the translation of scientific discoveries into improved diagnostics, prognostics, and patient-specific therapeutic strategies.

Accelerate

cancer research

Model first, experiment second — narrowing the hypothesis space before anything reaches the bench.

Reduce

cost & time of drug development

Predictive models cut the number of experiments needed to reach a decision.

Advance

precision medicine

Molecular subtyping and treatment-response prediction, tuned to Indian cohorts.

Improve

survival & quality of life

Durable control strategies rather than maximal short-term response.

Equitable

& data-driven healthcare

Solutions built to be affordable and clinically usable at national scale.

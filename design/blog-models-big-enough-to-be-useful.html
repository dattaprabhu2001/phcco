Models big enough to be useful, small enough to be understood

MB Marc Birtwistle · 18 February 2026 · 7 min read

There is a persistent tension in mechanistic modelling of cell signalling. Small models are transparent: you can hold the whole thing in your head, reason about which parameter matters, and explain the result to a biologist in a single figure. But they leave out most of the network, and the parts they leave out are frequently the parts that determine drug response.

Large models fix the coverage problem and create a new one. Once a model has several hundred species and a thousand rate constants, the honest answer to "why did it predict that?" becomes "because of the fit", which is not an answer a clinician can act on.

What we have found useful is to treat model size as a design decision rather than an aspiration — to ask, for a given question about a given drug, what is the smallest network that still contains the mechanism we are arguing about. That question has a different answer for every project, which is uncomfortable, but it produces models people trust.

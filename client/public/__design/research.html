Research

Three themes, one question: how do cancers change?

Our work integrates mathematical models with experimental and clinical data to explain how tumours develop, evolve and resist treatment — and where that leaves them vulnerable.

Phenotypic Plasticity Regulatory Networks Tumour Ecosystems
01

Phenotypic Plasticity and Cellular Heterogeneity

How cells switch between functional states — and how that drives metastasis and drug resistance.

Cellular phenotypic plasticity enables cells to dynamically transition between multiple functional states in response to intrinsic regulatory programs and extracellular cues. We investigate the molecular and systems-level mechanisms governing epithelial–mesenchymal plasticity (EMP), hybrid cell states and developmental reprogramming across multiple cancer types. By integrating mathematical models with experimental and clinical data, we seek to understand how non-genetic heterogeneity emerges, how it influences tumour progression, metastasis, therapeutic resistance and immune interactions, and how these dynamic cell-state transitions can be therapeutically manipulated.

Focus areas

  • Epithelial–mesenchymal plasticity
  • Hybrid cell states
  • Non-genetic heterogeneity
  • Metastasis
  • Therapeutic resistance
02

Gene Regulatory Networks and Cancer Cell-State Dynamics

How regulatory networks shape cellular identity, plasticity and tumour behaviour.

Cancer cell phenotypes emerge from the collective dynamics of interconnected gene regulatory networks rather than the activity of individual genes. We develop mechanistic and mathematical models to characterize regulatory circuits, feedback structures, multistability and dynamical transitions that govern cell-state decisions. By integrating network models with transcriptomic and phenotypic data, we aim to identify regulatory architectures underlying cellular heterogeneity, state stability and plasticity, and to understand how perturbations of these networks drive tumour progression and therapeutic adaptation.

Focus areas

  • Multistability
  • Network topology
  • Dynamical landscapes
  • Phenotypic robustness
  • Stem-cell biology
03

Tumour Ecosystems, Evolution and Therapeutic Strategies

Tumours as ecosystems — and the ecological vulnerabilities that better therapy can exploit.

Tumours function as evolving ecosystems where cancer cells continuously interact with stromal, immune and metabolic components of the tumour microenvironment. Our research investigates how ecological interactions, spatial heterogeneity, hypoxia, metabolic competition and microenvironmental signalling shape tumour evolution, invasion and treatment response. Through multiscale mathematical modelling and systems-level analyses, we study adaptive therapy, tumour–immune dynamics and microenvironment-driven plasticity to identify vulnerabilities that can improve combination therapies and guide the development of more durable precision oncology strategies.

Focus areas

  • Tumour microenvironment
  • Adaptive therapy
  • Tumour–immune dynamics
  • Hypoxia
  • Multiscale modelling
How we work

Methods that cut across every theme

PHCCO brings together mathematics, biology, medicine, engineering and computation — applying these six capabilities to all three research themes.

Systems biology

Mechanistic models of regulatory circuits and signalling networks.

Machine learning

Predictive models over multi-omic and imaging data.

Multi-omics

Genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics, integrated.

Mathematical modelling

ODE, agent-based and multiscale formulations of tumour dynamics.

Digital pathology

Quantitative readouts from histology and spatial data.

Epigenomics

Methylation and chromatin state as drivers of plasticity.
